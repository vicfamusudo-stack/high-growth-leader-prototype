02 · The core idea

Ask one revealing question

“If you were away from your role for 30 days, what would stop working?”

This is not a challenge to disappear or abandon responsibility. It is a diagnostic thought experiment. It reveals where a team relies on one person's memory, judgement, authority or relationships instead of a reliable system.

The aim is not to make the leader unnecessary. It is to make the work more resilient and free the leader to focus on decisions and development that genuinely need their attention.

03 · Four risk areas

Look in four places

PEOPLE

Who lacks the capability or confidence to act without you?

PROCESS

What is undocumented, inconsistent or fragile?

DECISIONS

Which routine choices cannot move without your approval?

KNOWLEDGE

What important judgement exists only in your head?

04 · Worked example

An agency director becomes the bottleneck

A growing agency delivers strong work, but the director remains involved in too many routine decisions.

RiskCategoryWhy it mattersFirst transfer action
Routine client changes wait for approvalDecisionsWork queues; the team learns to escalateDefine decision limits and exceptions
Only the director can scope complex workKnowledgeNew projects stall when the director is busyRecord two worked examples and a scoping checklist
No one owns weekly capacity planningProcessConflicts surface too lateAssign an owner and a weekly review standard

Notice: the answer is not “delegate everything”. Make the standard visible, set guardrails, transfer authority in stages and inspect evidence.

06 · Your risk register

Score and prioritise

Give each risk an impact score and dependency score from 1 (low) to 5 (high). Priority = impact × dependency. Use the score to support judgement, not replace it.

07 · Knowledge check

What should the director do first?

08 · Seven-day experiment

Transfer one capability safely

Choose one risk. Define a bounded experiment, a named owner, guardrails and evidence. Keep an escalation route for exceptional or high-consequence situations.
